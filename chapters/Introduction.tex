\section{Motivation}

Foodborne diseases are often underrated, despite the elevated number of yearly
cases, yet they frequently fail to capture public interest beyond isolated
incidents; recent cases that came to light in Italy involve the hospitalization
and death of children and pregnant women following ingestion of E.coli
contaminated foods (\ie{} raw milk products and chicken)
\citep{EcoliBimbo2024,EcoliMamma2025}. Regulatory agencies, including the United
States Food and Drug Administration (US FDA), the European Food Safety Authority
(EFSA), and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), implement
rigid measures and rigorous testing to prevent contamination and maintain
quality over the food chain; however, these measures can only guarantee food
safety up to a certain point, namely during production and distribution, but
with limited control once food enters our homes. Current pathogen and toxin
detection systems are primarily employed for occasional checks or for
post-outbreaks investigations. The need for more accessible and widely
distributed systems still remains unmet. The motivation for this PhD project
stems from the necessity to develop simple, cost-effective tools that can be
used by everyone to check for pathogens or food toxins, mirroring the increasing
accessibility of diagnostics in the medical field, such as the inexpensive,
rapid, and user-friendly lateral flow sensors most commonly used for COVID-19
diagnostics and pregnancy tests.

\section{Thesis aim}

The objective of this thesis is to develop a stable, sensitive sensor for
foodborne hazards. This sensor aims to address the need for rapid and reliable
detection of contaminants in food, ultimately contributing to improved food
safety and public health. Current methods for detecting foodborne pathogens and
toxins, such as traditional culturing techniques and enzyme-linked immunosorbent
assays (ELISA), can be time-consuming, labor-intensive, and may lack the
sensitivity required to detect low levels of contamination. For example,
culturing Salmonella can take up to 72 hours, delaying crucial intervention
measures. ELISA, while faster than cultures, requires specialized equipment and
advanced knowledge. Therefore, a sensor offering faster, more sensitive, and
potentially portable detection capabilities would represent a significant
advancement.

While the performance of sensors has been greatly improved since their
development, their stability still needs to be addressed. Stability refers to
the ability f the sensor to maintain its performance characteristics, namely
sensitivity, selectivity, and accuracy, over extended periods of time and under
varying environmental conditions, such as temperature and humidity fluctuations,
which are common in food processing and storage environments. It is imperative
that the performance of the sensor remains reliable, in order to properly detect
low concentrations of foodborne hazards, enabling early detection and prevention
of outbreaks. This is particularly important for toxins like aflatoxin, where
even trace amounts can pose a significant health risk.

Indeed, this thesis proves that a sensor can be fabricated using an
electrolyte-gated field effect transistor (EG-FET) that has been stabilized by
using a lipophilic membrane on the carbon-nanotube-based channel. EG-FETs are
particularly attractive for biosensing thanks to their label-free detection
capabilities and potential for miniaturization. A lipophilic membrane, polyvinyl
chloride (PVC)-based, is added to improve the long-term stability of the sensor
in complex biological media. To prove this, the gate of the device was
functionalized with two different recognition elements: first an ion-selective
membrane to detect ammonium ion, then aptamers for histamine. The ammonium
ion-selective membrane incorporates the nonactin ionophore, which selectively
binds ammonium ions, creating a potential difference at the membrane-electrolyte
interface, modulating the current of the transistor. Histamine, on the other
hand, was detected using aptamers, which are single-stranded DNA molecules that
bind their specific target with high affinity. While these toxins are not
lethal, unlike botulinum toxin or aflatoxins, they prove that our platform can
work with different analytes, demonstrating the versatility of the sensor
design. The successful detection of both a small ion (ammonium) and a larger
organic molecule (histamine) highlights the adaptability of the EG-FET platform
for a wide range of sensing applications.

\section{Scientific collaborations}

The work herein presented is highly multidisciplinary, demanding expertise in
electronic engineering, physics, chemistry, and biology. For this reason, a
collaboration with Dr. Stefano Bonaldo and Prof. Alessandro Paccagnella from the
Department of Information Engineering at the University of Padova. This
collaboration provided additional information regarding the protective membrane
and helped to select optimal characterization experiments that could be
independently conducted in Bolzano. Furthermore, a collaboration was established
with the Theory and Modelling group at Linköping University, led by Prof. Igor
Zozoulenko, to develop a mathematical model describing operational mechanism of
the EG-FET and to conduct simulations for a deeper understanding of its
functionality.

\section{Thesis outline}

After the initial introduction, Chapter \ref{cap:chapter1} introduces the
background, discussing foodborne illnesses and their current detection methods,
highlighting their limitations, such as their speed or sensitivity, and
proposing a solution using sensors and biosensors. Finally, it includes a
section on relevant mathematical models and simulations used to support the
experimental work. Chapter \ref{cap:chapter2} describes the materials and
methods employed in the experiments to achieve the results presented in
subsequent chapters. Chapter \ref{cap:chapter3} presents the work conducted to
accelerate and enhance the stability of the devices. Chapter \ref{cap:chapter4}
focuses on the mathematical model describing the EG-FET's operation, including
preliminary simulation results that require further optimization to match
experimental findings. Lastly, Chapter \ref{cap:chapter5} demonstrates the
functionality of the device employed as a signal transducer for ammonium and
histamine sensors, showcasing its versatility.
