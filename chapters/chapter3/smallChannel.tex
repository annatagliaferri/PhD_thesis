Further tests were conducted by developing a new geometry. A scaled-down
EG-CNTFET structure was designed with a channel and gate area reduced four times
compared to the standard devices; the structure will be referred to as nEG-FET
in the rest of the section. The nEG-FET was designed with a \SI{3}{\mm} $\times$
\SI{3}{\mm} gate and a \SI{1.5}{\mm} $\times$ \SI{1.5}{\mm} channel. The
distance between the gate and the channel was scaled in proportion with the
standard EG-CNTFET, amounting to \SI{0.75}{\mm}. The hypothesis to be tested was
that fewer CNTs would take less time to reach stability, making subsequent
sensing measurements faster, and would better maintain the stability achieved.
Nevertheless, one important thing to note is that the fraction of capacitance
attributed to the geometry ($C_{geom}$) is proportional to the gate area ($A$):

\begin{equation}
    C_{geom} \approx \varepsilon \dfrac{A}{d}
\end{equation}

where $d$ is dielectric thickness. It follows that a smaller area contributes to
a lower capacitance, which will inevitably lead to a lower transconductance and
sensitivity. These experiments were therefore considered preliminary, to test
the scalability and feasibility of miniaturization, while maintaining acceptable
performance for practical sensing applications.

Photolithography and electrode deposition were carried out maintaining the same
parameters as the previous two structures; on the other hand, the CNTs had to be
optimized to obtain a working device. Indeed, it was assumed to adopt the same
optimal working \rds{} range as for the larger structures, maintaining a target
between \SI{1}{\kohm} and \SI{10}{\kohm}. The main change that occurred was the
number of CNT layers required: it was found that \num{48} CNT layers were
sufficient to achieve the previously-defined range, resulting in functional
devices.

The performance of the devices and their stabilizing capabilities were assessed
as before, through the collection of transfer and output characteristics, the
extraction of \ion{}, and the calculation of ON/OFF ratio and \vth{}. 

Figure \ref{fig:nEGFETData} displays the transfer characteristics and the
normalized current for two representative devices, showing how nEG-FETs only
found stability in some instances, which is insufficient for reliable,
consistent sensing performance. Figures \ref{fig:nTransferDown} and
\ref{fig:nNormalizedDown} show a device that failed to stabilize, with a worse
performance than the standard EG-CNTFET, exhibiting a current that continued to
worsen over time. The second device, shown in Figures \ref{fig:nTransferUp} and
\ref{fig:nNormalizedUp}, did find a level of stability, as a \emph{constant
slope phase} with increasing current is visible. Another important issue that
does not have to be overlooked is the fact that when these devices did
stabilize, they did with great variability, as stabilization occurred on average
at \SI{25.25}{\pm 11.32}{min}. Despite this, a full characterization of the
devices was performed to gain more insight into the performance of nEG-FETs.

\SI{0}{\V} by the end of the collection of transfer curves.

It is therefore possible to note that the pattern for these parameters are
similar throughout all the geometries tested.

The performance of the nEG-FET devices, as analyzed through the \ratio{} and
\vth{} in Figure \ref{fig:avgNParams}, presents mixed results. The \ratio{} is
the worst among all tested devices, probably due to the reduced number of CNTs
in the channel, which leads to a lower overall current and a less pronounced
off-state. On the other hand, \vth{} is the lowest among the tested devices. One
reason behind this different behaviour could be ascribed to the fact that even
small defects have a larger impact on the small gate, for instance, if the
membrane is not perfectly uniform, the EDLs might be locally thinner; this would
cause a larger EDL capacitance, which would translate to a lower \vth{}. Another
possible explanation could be that with fewer CNTs, less carriers are needed
before conduction. While this lower \vth{} could be seen as an advantage in
terms of power efficiency, the overall poor performance of the \ratio{} suggests
that this geometry is not ideal for sensor applications.
