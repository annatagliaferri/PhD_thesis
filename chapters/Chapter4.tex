\begin{color}{teal}
Building on the previously described fabrication and characterization of a stable and high-performance electrolyte-gated carbon nanotube field-effect transistor (EG-CNTFET), this chapter explores its application as a versatile transducer for chemical sensing. The unique electrical properties of the device, combined with appropriate surface functionalization, allow selective detection of target analytes, enabling the investigation of both small ionic species and biologically relevant molecules. The chapter is structured as follows: first, general strategies for EG-CNTFET functionalization are discussed; second, the device is applied to the detection of ammonium ions; and finally, histamine detection is explored, illustrating the potential of the platform for broader sensing applications.
\end{color}

\section{Conclusion}

The stabilized EG-CNTFET platform can be successfully used as a signal transducer in biosensors and is compatible with multiple detection elements. Functionalization with an ion-sensitive membrane resulted in the detection of ammonium in a linear manner ($R^2=0.94$) with a sensitivity of \SI{0.14}{\uA \per decade} at concentrations between \SIrange{0.01}{100}{mM}.
Functionalization with aptamers, on the other hand, detected histamine with a linear trend($R^2=0.95$) in concentrations between \SIrange{0.01}{100}{\micro M}, with a sensitivity of \SI{0.15}{\uA \per decade}.
